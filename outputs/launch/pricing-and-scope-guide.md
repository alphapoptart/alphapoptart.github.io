### Pricing rules

- Quote the scope, format, estimated time, and price before work starts.
- Pause and ask for approval before extra time, extra devices, travel beyond the agreed area, or a new problem.
- Same-day or evening appointments can carry a clearly stated $25 convenience fee; never add it after the booking is accepted.
- Payment is due at the end of the session by the method you publish. Do not collect card numbers, bank details, PINs, or passwords in chat, email, or notes.
- A 24-hour cancellation policy is a reasonable starting point: one first-time late cancellation is waived; later late cancellations may be charged 50% of the reserved session. Put the final policy in the customer agreement and follow local rules.
- If the issue is unsafe, regulated, outside your skill, or materially different from the agreed scope, stop and refer rather than improvising.

## Services and information Northstar will not handle

The safest rule is: **the customer keeps control of sensitive accounts and documents; Sean does not receive, store, interpret, transfer, or make decisions with them.**

### Financial and money movement

- No logging into or operating bank, brokerage, retirement, lending, payment-processor, credit-card, payroll, tax, cryptocurrency, gambling, or money-transfer accounts.
- No initiating, approving, moving, withdrawing, depositing, or reconciling money.
- No entering or transcribing account numbers, routing numbers, card numbers, PINs, security answers, Social Security numbers, or tax IDs.
- No bookkeeping, tax preparation, investment, credit, debt, insurance, or financial advice.
- No handling checks, cash, payment cards, financial statements, or cryptocurrency wallets.

### Medical, health, and personal-care information

- No accessing, downloading, organizing, interpreting, transmitting, or storing medical records, prescriptions, patient-portal content, insurance claims, lab results, or care plans.
- No medical, mental-health, medication, benefits, or treatment advice.
- No work that would require Northstar to receive protected health information on behalf of a covered healthcare organization unless a qualified professional has reviewed the arrangement and any required written agreement is in place.

### Identity, legal, and government records

- No handling passports, driver's licenses, immigration files, court records, legal case files, adoption records, or identity-verification documents.
- No legal advice, form interpretation, representation, notarization, benefits determination, or government filing on a customer's behalf.
- No signing, consenting, attesting, or making a statement as the customer.

### Credentials, surveillance, and unauthorized access

- No receiving, copying, storing, or entering passwords, one-time codes, recovery codes, private keys, or biometric data.
- No bypassing passwords, two-factor authentication, device locks, parental controls, software licensing, paywalls, or access controls.
- No spyware, stalkerware, hidden monitoring, employee surveillance, location tracking, or account takeover.
- No accessing an employer, school, family member's, or former partner's system without the account owner's clear authorization.
- No unattended remote access. Any screen share is temporary, visible, consent-based, and ended before the session closes.

### High-risk technical work

- No guaranteed data recovery, forensic investigation, breach response, malware eradication, penetration testing, or evidence preservation.
- No electrical, gas, structural, medical-device, alarm, or life-safety equipment work.
- No work that could destroy evidence, void a warranty, violate a license, or put a person or system at risk.
- No promises that a device, account, network, or data can be fixed, recovered, secured, or made compliant.

## Session safety rules

1. The customer or authorized account owner stays present and enters all credentials themselves.
2. Get explicit consent before screen sharing, changing settings, accessing a file, or connecting a device.
3. Collect the minimum information needed; do not copy sensitive documents into Northstar notes.
4. Keep notes factual and operational: date, scope, actions, customer-approved next step.
5. Stop immediately if fraud, abuse, a data breach, a medical emergency, a legal issue, or unauthorized access is suspected.
6. Refer to the relevant bank, healthcare provider, attorney, CPA, licensed security professional, manufacturer, or emergency service using contact information the customer verifies independently.
7. Never ask a customer to send a password, one-time code, full financial number, medical record, or identity document by email or text.

## Before accepting a job

- Confirm the customer's identity and authority to work on the device or account.
- Confirm the exact scope, price, format, travel area, cancellation terms, and what will not be handled.
- Confirm the customer understands that outcomes are not guaranteed and that they remain responsible for backups, account ownership, and decisions.
- Use a written customer agreement and privacy notice reviewed for your location.
- Carry appropriate general/professional liability coverage and ask an insurance professional whether cyber coverage is relevant.

## Why these boundaries are conservative

Federal guidance treats certain data-handling relationships as higher-risk. HHS explains that a person performing services involving protected health information for a covered entity can be a “business associate,” with written-assurance and safeguarding obligations. The FTC recommends small businesses minimize sensitive data, protect what they keep, and avoid asking customers for information by phone or email when it is not needed. This guide avoids those relationships by default; it does not determine whether a law applies to a particular engagement.
